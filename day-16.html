Day 16 — The Myth of Progress

"No idea is as absurd as the idea of progress." — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

The modern world rests on a foundational belief that history moves upward by necessity. Each generation is assumed to stand higher than the last. Technology, comfort, and the expansion of rights are taken as proof that the movement is real and irreversible. This belief is so deeply installed that questioning it feels, to most people, like questioning reality itself.

Tradition works with a different map. History is cyclical. Civilizations rise when qualitative and spiritual forces are strong. They decline when those forces weaken and quantitative, material forces take their place. The present age is understood as a late phase of descent, not as a summit. What is called progress is often the further advance of forces that dissolve higher forms of life and replace them with more efficient but lower ones.

You will feel the resistance of this idea. Every institution that claims to educate young men teaches the opposite story. The pressure to accept the progressive myth is constant and rarely examined. Clear thinking begins by separating the undeniable gains in technique and material power from the claim that these gains equal a higher state of the human type. The two are not the same.

Once the myth of automatic upward movement is set aside, many of the demands placed on you by the age lose their automatic authority. You are no longer required to treat every change as improvement simply because it is new. The standard returns to quality, form, and the presence or absence of a vertical orientation. That shift in vision is the beginning of a real diagnosis.
