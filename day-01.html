Day 1 — What Tradition Means

"Tradition in its fullest sense, as distinct from mere traditionalism, is a category belonging to a world that has almost disappeared, to times when a single educational force manifested itself in customs as well as in faith, in law as well as in political forms and culture." — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

Most people hear the word Tradition and picture old customs, family habits, or political nostalgia. That is the common meaning. It is also the weakest one.

In the sense used here, Tradition names a vertical orientation of life. It is the recognition that there are higher and lower ways of existing, and that the higher ones must shape the lower ones. It points upward. A society can be full of old forms and still be cut off from this orientation. A man living in the most modern conditions can still hold to it. The age you are born into does not decide the matter. Your stance does.

You will already have been taught the opposite view in almost every setting that claims authority over young men. School, media, and much of ordinary conversation treat hierarchy as suspicion and the idea of higher forms of life as dangerous. When you first meet the Traditional view it can feel like a foreign language. That reaction is normal. The surrounding culture has spent decades making the vertical dimension seem either absurd or immoral. Clear thinking begins by noticing that the discomfort is produced by the environment, not by the idea itself.

These pages are not a call to restore the past. They are an attempt to recover a way of seeing that the modern world has almost completely lost. The work starts with orientation. Everything that follows depends on it.
