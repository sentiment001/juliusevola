Day 292 — The Demand That Does Not Dissolve

"The demand of the higher type does not dissolve with the dissolution of the world that once recognized it. It continues as the interior claim upon the man who still hears it." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

The demand that does not dissolve is the permanent claim of the higher type upon the man who still recognizes it. The residual man continues to answer that demand even though the age has dissolved every collective form that once enforced it. The demand itself remains intact. Only the collective enforcement that once made it public has disappeared.

Young men who still feel the pressure of a higher claim often look for residual authorities or residual communities that will keep the demand alive for them. Most of those authorities have already been emptied by the horizontal forces that dominate the age. The residual path does not wait for external enforcement. It continues as the permanent answer from the interior center that still hears the claim.

This solitary answer is the residual form of the older traditional response to a higher law. The man who continues to answer the demand already performs the residual act. That act does not require collective recognition to remain real.

Answer the demand. Do not wait for the age to restore conditions in which the higher claim is publicly enforced. The residual act is to keep the permanent demand decisive in every concrete decision. That decisiveness is the continuity of the higher type under residual conditions.
