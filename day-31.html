Day 31 — Materialism as the Ruling Metaphysics

"Materialism is not merely a philosophical theory. It is the practical metaphysics of the modern world, the assumption that only what can be measured, produced, and consumed has full reality." — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

Materialism operates as the unspoken framework of the age even when it is not formally defended. Reality is identified with the physical, the measurable, and the economically productive. Higher claims—spiritual, hierarchical, or metaphysical—are either reduced to material causes or dismissed as subjective. The result is a contraction of the field of the real.

This framework shapes education, medicine, politics, and private life. The body is treated as a machine to be optimized. The mind is treated as a product of neurological processes. The social order is treated as a system of material incentives and constraints. Within this horizon the traditional idea of a vertical dimension becomes literally unintelligible to many.

The young man who still senses that something essential is missing will find that the official languages of the age offer no vocabulary for that sense. He is told that his restlessness is a psychological problem or a residual superstition. The diagnosis of materialism allows him to see that the problem is not in him but in the reduced ontology of the world he has inherited.

Rejection of materialism does not require the adoption of any particular religious system. It requires the decision to treat the higher as real and to form oneself accordingly. That decision is the first step out of the ruling metaphysics of the age.
