Remaining Standing

Residual Application Sheet
"To remain standing is already the residual form of higher continuity when every collective expression of that continuity has been dissolved." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

Remaining standing is the residual posture once every external confirmation has been removed. In earlier ages standing was supported by rank, by rite, by the visible presence of a higher order that made the upright position ordinary. Those supports have been dissolved. What remains is the man who continues to orient himself by the permanent measure even though the age offers no shared recognition and no collective form that rewards the orientation. The standing itself does not become less real because it is no longer mirrored. It becomes residual. The man who keeps it already performs the act of continuity that the outer structures can no longer perform.

Young men who still sense the need to stand often look for residual movements, residual ranks, or residual company that will give the posture external weight. Most of what they find has already been reshaped by the horizontal forces of the age. The search itself can become a way of postponing the solitary requirement. The residual path does not wait for favorable conditions or for companions who will confirm the measure. It continues from the interior center that remains available. Solitude in this domain is not a preference. It is the condition under which the standing either proves itself or is abandoned in favor of a more comfortable alignment.

The practice is the continuation of one higher demand in a situation that offers no recognition and no company. The demand is chosen from the domains already examined: the ordering of the body, the measure of speech, the command of a desire, the protection of time, or the quality of work. It is maintained in the precise setting where external confirmation is absent and where the easier movement would be to relax the standard. The duration is long enough for the absence of recognition to be felt. The practice ends only when the demand has been held without revision. Repeated, it trains the man to treat the lack of external support as the ordinary terrain rather than as an excuse.

This solitary standing is the residual form of the older traditional posture. The collective structures that once made higher standing visible have disappeared. The man who continues to remain upright according to the permanent measure already keeps the continuity that those structures can no longer guarantee. The act does not require a horizon in which it will be publicly restored. It requires only that the interior orientation not be surrendered because the age has ceased to reward it.
