The Northern-Atlantic Cycle

“As far as the migration of the Northern primordial race is concerned, it is necessary to distinguish two great waves, the first moving from north to south, the second from west to east. Groups of Hyperboreans carrying the same spirit, the same blood, and the same body of symbols, signs, and languages first reached North America and the northern regions of the Eurasian continent.” Revolt Against the Modern World

In the emigration of the Boreal race it is necessary to distinguish two great currents: one going from the north to the south, the other, later, from the west to the east. Bearing the same spirit, the same blood, and the same system of symbols, signs, and words, groups of Hyperboreans first reached North America and the northern regions of the Eurasian continent. After several tens of thousands of years a second great wave of emigration advanced as far as Central America but concentrated above all in a region, today disappeared, situated in the Atlantic area, where it constituted a center in the image of the polar center. This corresponds to the Atlantis of the accounts of Plato and Diodorus. This displacement and reconstitution explain the interferences of names, symbols, and topographies that characterize the memories relative to the first two ages. It is therefore essentially of a Nordic-Atlantic race and civilization that one must speak. From the Atlantic region the races of the second cycle would have radiated into America (whence the already mentioned memories of the Nahua, the Toltecs, and the Aztecs concerning their original homeland), as well as into Europe and Africa. It is very probable that in the early Paleolithic these races reached western Europe.

Originally the Atlantic center was supposed to reproduce the polar function of the Hyperborean seat, and this circumstance is the source of frequent interferences in the matter of traditions and memories. These interferences should not prevent one from detecting, in a later period still belonging to the highest prehistory, a transformation of civilization and of spirituality, a differentiation that marks the passage from the first era to the second (from the Golden Age to the Silver Age) and opens the way to the third era, the Bronze Age or Titanic Age. One could even, strictly speaking, qualify the latter as Atlantean, since the Hellenic tradition presents Atlas, as the brother of Prometheus, as a figure related to the Titans. The legend of Atlas holding the world on his shoulders, while under one aspect corresponding to the punishment of the Titan who according to some participated in the struggle against the Olympians, under another aspect may serve to symbolize the polar regency itself, the function of pole, of support or spiritual axis that, after the Hyperboreans, the Atlantic people in a first period would have assumed. The freezing and the long night descended at a given moment on the polar region. The emigration that resulted from these facts marked the end of the first cycle and the inauguration of the second, the beginning of the second great era, the Atlantic cycle.

From the anthropological as from the spiritual point of view one must therefore distinguish two components, one Boreal and the other Atlantic, in the vast matter of the traditions and institutions of this second cycle. One is linked directly to the light of the North, maintaining in great part the original Uranian and polar orientation. The other denounces the transformation that occurred in contact with the powers of the South. The races that issued from the Atlantic seat carried the memory of a center modeled on the original polar one, yet already conditioned by the passage into a new era. In America the white and solar Quetzalcoatl arrives from the land situated beyond the waters. In Ireland the Tuatha de Danann come from the western island of Avalon, guided by the hero of sunny countenance. The same body of symbols, the same solar and polar references, reappear, yet the pure state of the Hyperborean origins has undergone the first differentiation. The Silver Age is this intermediate condition: the polar function is still assumed, but the mixture and the contact with lower elements have begun to modify the form.

The Northern-Atlantic cycle therefore stands between the pure Hyperborean seat and the later Titanic and heroic cycles. It is the era in which the original light of the North is still present as a directing principle, yet already subject to the conditioning that will open the way to the Bronze Age. The interferences of names and topographies between Arctic and Atlantic memories are the natural result of the second center having been constituted in the image of the first. To hold this cycle is to recognize the precise historical and spiritual passage from the age of Being to the age in which the human and contingent element begins its progressive intrusion. Without that recognition the subsequent cycles of decadence remain unanchored, and the revolt against the modern world loses the intermediate measure that still preserves a memory of the polar origin.
