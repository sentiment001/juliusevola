Tradition and Antitradition

“In the modern world one does not encounter merely the absence of the traditional spirit, but the presence of an antitraditional spirit that organizes the process of dissolution.” Revolt Against the Modern World

Beyond the residual forms of the heroic cycle the process of involution reaches the point at which simple decay is no longer sufficient to account for the character of the age. What appears is an active antitradition. This is not the mere fading of higher principles or the persistence of lower residues. It is the systematic elevation of the forces of the lower nature and the organized inversion of the hierarchical order that once oriented existence toward the higher. In civilizations that remained outside the full trajectory of the Western modern spirit, traditional structures often continued to hold. Hierarchical forms, sacred authority, and the recognition of higher and lower natures persisted in various degrees until the moment of contact with the modern West. The East, the remaining traditional societies of Africa and the Americas, and other non-Western formations retained, for longer periods, the imprint of an order that still measured life by a vertical standard.

The West, having carried the process of rationalization, secularization, and materialization further than any other civilization, becomes the decisive carrier of the antitraditional current. What had been a gradual conditioning by lunar, telluric, and Dionysian elements is now accelerated and directed by a spirit that actively opposes the traditional orientation. Quantity is elevated over quality. The lower functions of the social body claim sovereignty over the higher. Symbols that once expressed a vertical order are retained only as emptied forms or are inverted so that they serve the forces of dissolution. The antitradition does not simply ignore the traditional principles. It organizes against them, substituting counterfeit unities for organic hierarchy and horizontal equality for the recognition of rank and function. In this phase the modern world is no longer a civilization among others. It is the concrete historical form of the antitraditional spirit.

The demand the chapter places on the reader is therefore exact. To recognize Tradition and Antitradition is to hold the distinction between residual forms that still carry some measure of the higher orientation and the active force that now directs the dissolution. The residual is what remains of an earlier order. The antitraditional is what organizes the lower against the higher and spreads the process beyond the West. Without that distinction the later history of the modern world remains a series of unanchored events, and the revolt against the modern world has no precise measure of the force that must be met in the present age.
