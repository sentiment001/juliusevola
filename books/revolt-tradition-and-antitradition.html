Tradition and Antitradition

“In the modern world one does not encounter merely the absence of the traditional spirit, but the presence of an antitraditional spirit that organizes the process of dissolution.” Revolt Against the Modern World

After the heroic cycle the process of involution no longer remains a matter of successive conditionings by lunar, telluric, or Dionysian elements. A further stage appears. What had been residual forms of an earlier order, still carrying some measure of hierarchical recognition and sacred orientation, now faces an active antitradition. This is not the simple fading of higher principles or the survival of lower residues. It is the organized elevation of the forces belonging to the lower nature and the systematic inversion of the vertical standard that once measured existence. In this phase the distinction between what remains of Tradition and what actively opposes it becomes decisive. Residual tradition is what persists of an earlier structure. Antitradition is the force that now directs the dissolution and spreads it.

Civilizations that remained outside the full trajectory of the Western modern spirit retained traditional structures for longer periods. In the East, in residual societies of Africa and the Americas, and in other non-Western formations, hierarchical forms, sacred authority, and the recognition of higher and lower natures continued to hold in varying degrees until the moment of decisive contact with the modern West. These were not pure survivals of the Golden Age. They were already conditioned forms. Yet they still measured life by a vertical standard rather than by the horizontal and quantitative criteria that define the modern age. The persistence of caste, of sacred kingship, of ritual order, and of the distinction between higher and lower functions marked them as still belonging, in their essential orientation, to the world of Tradition even after the heroic cycle had closed in the West.

The West, having carried the process of rationalization, secularization, and materialization further than any other civilization, becomes the decisive carrier of the antitraditional current. What had been a gradual conditioning is now accelerated and directed by a spirit that actively opposes the traditional orientation. Quantity is elevated over quality. The lower functions of the social body claim sovereignty over the higher. The merchant and the mass displace the warrior and the sacred authority. Symbols that once expressed a vertical order are retained only as emptied forms or are inverted so that they serve the forces of dissolution. The antitradition does not simply ignore the traditional principles. It organizes against them, substituting counterfeit unities for organic hierarchy and horizontal equality for the recognition of rank and function. In this phase the modern world is no longer one civilization among others. It is the concrete historical form of the antitraditional spirit, and through its expansion it accelerates the dissolution of residual traditional forms wherever they still stand.

The demand the chapter places on the reader is therefore exact. To recognize Tradition and Antitradition is to hold the distinction between residual forms that still carry some measure of the higher orientation and the active force that now directs the dissolution. The residual is what remains of an earlier order and can still be measured against it. The antitraditional is what organizes the lower against the higher, elevates quantity, and spreads the process beyond the West. Without that distinction the later history of the modern world remains a series of unanchored events, and the revolt against the modern world has no precise measure of the force that must be met in the present age. The subsequent chapters will examine the specifically Western cycle in which this antitraditional spirit took historical form.
