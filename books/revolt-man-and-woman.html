Man and Woman

“In traditional symbolism, the supernatural principle was conceived as masculine and the principle of nature and of becoming as feminine.” Revolt Against the Modern World

To complete the considerations on traditional life one must address the world of sex. Here too the traditional conception maintains correspondences between realities and symbols and between actions and rites. From these correspondences the principles were drawn for understanding the sexes and for the relations that must be established between man and woman in every normal civilization. The supernatural principle was conceived as masculine; the principle of nature and of becoming as feminine. In Hellenic terms the One (to en), that which is in itself, complete and sufficient, is masculine; the dyad, principle of the different and of the other-than-self, and therefore of desire and movement, is feminine. In Hindu terms (Sāmkhya) the impassible spirit (puruṣa) is masculine and prakṛti, the active matrix of every conditioned form, is feminine. The Far Eastern tradition expressed equivalent concepts in the cosmic duality of yang and yin: yang, the masculine principle, is associated with the virtue of Heaven; yin, the feminine principle, with that of the Earth.

Considered in themselves the two principles stand in opposition. In the order of the creative, triumphal formation that has repeatedly been identified as the soul of the traditional world, and that develops historically in relation to the conflict between races and civilizations, they become elements of a synthesis in which each retains a distinct function. Behind the various figurations of the myth of the fall one often finds the idea of the masculine principle identifying itself with and losing itself in the feminine principle until it passes to the mode of being of the latter. When that which by nature is a principle in itself, opening itself to the forces of desire, succumbs to the law of that which does not have its own principle in itself, one must speak of a fall. On the plane of human reality this is the basis of the diffidence shown by various traditions toward woman, frequently considered a principle of sin, impurity, and evil, a temptation and a danger for the one who turns toward the supernatural.

To the direction of the fall another possibility can be opposed: the just relation. It is established when the feminine principle, whose nature is to refer itself to another, turns not toward that which again flees but toward a masculine stability. Then a limit appears. Stability is transmitted, and to such a degree that it intimately transfigures every feminine possibility. In these terms one has a synthesis in the positive sense. What is required is a conversion of the feminine so that it exists entirely for the opposite principle, and above all that the latter remain absolutely and integrally itself. Then, in metaphysical symbols, the woman becomes the bride who is also the power, the instrumental generating force that receives from the immobile male the first principle of motion and of form, according to the doctrine of śakti already indicated, which can also be found, differently expressed, in Aristotelianism and Neoplatonism. The Tantric-Tibetan symbolic figurations are significant in this regard: the male bearer-of-the-scepter is immobile, cold, and made of light, while the śakti that envelops him and has him as axis is substantiated with mobile flame.

These meanings form the basis of the traditional norm concerning the sexes in the concrete sense. The norm obeys the principle of the regime of castes and therefore refers to the two pivots of dharma and of bhakti or fides: own nature and active dedication. If birth is not chance, it is not chance that one awakens to oneself in a male or female body. The physical difference is to be conceived as the correspondence of a spiritual difference; one is man or woman physically only because one is so transcendentally, and the characteristic of sex, far from being irrelevant with regard to the spirit, is the sign that indicates a way, a distinct dharma. The will to order and to form constitutes the basis of every traditional civilization. Traditional law does not push toward the unqualified, the equal, the indefinite (that in which the various parts of the whole become promiscuously or atomically similar) but wants those parts to be themselves and to express ever more perfectly their own nature. Thus, with regard to the sexes, man and woman present themselves as two types. The one born man must realize himself as man, the one born woman as woman, wholly and in every respect, overcoming every mixture and promiscuity. Even with regard to the supernatural direction, man and woman must each have their own way, which cannot be changed without falling into a contradictory and inorganic mode of being.

The mode of being that corresponds eminently to man has already been considered, as have the two principal forms of approach to the value of being in itself: Action and Contemplation. The Warrior (the Hero) and the Ascetic are therefore the two fundamental types of pure virility. In symmetry with them there are two for the feminine nature. The woman realizes herself as such and rises to the same level as the man as Warrior and as Ascetic in so far as she is Lover and in so far as she is Mother. These are bipartitions of the same ideal stock. As there is an active heroism there is also a negative one; there is the heroism of absolute affirmation and that of absolute dedication. One can be as luminous and as rich in fruits as the other on the plane of overcoming and of liberation when lived with purity and with the significance of an offering. Precisely this differentiation in the heroic stock determines the distinctive character of the ways of fulfillment for man and for woman as types. To the gesture of the Warrior and of the Ascetic who, the one by means of pure action and the other by means of pure detachment, affirm themselves in a life that is beyond life, there corresponds in the woman the gesture of giving herself entirely to another being, of being entirely for another being, whether the beloved man (type of the Lover, Aphrodistic woman) or the son (type of the Mother, Demetrian woman), finding in this the sense of her own life, her own joy, and her own justification. No other is the bhakti or fides that constitutes the normal and natural way of participation for the traditional woman, in the order of form and also, when lived absolutely and disindividually, beyond form. To realize oneself in an ever more decided way according to these two distinct and unmistakable directions, reducing in the woman everything that is man and in the man everything that is woman, tending toward the absolute man and the absolute woman: such is the traditional law for the sexes according to the various planes of life.

Traditionally, therefore, the woman could enter the sacred hierarchical order only mediately, through the relation to another, to the man. In India women, even of superior caste, did not have their own initiation; they belonged to the sacred community of the nobles (ārya) through their father before marriage and afterward through their husband, who was also the mystical head of the family. In Dorian Hellas and in Rome analogous determinations held: the woman stood under the potestas of the father or, after marriage, in manu viri, and was juridically regarded as filiae loco. These traditional decrees that regulate the dependency of the woman, far from being unjust or arrogant, defined the limits and the natural place of the only spiritual path proper to pure feminine nature. The modern woman has felt an entirely imaginary inferiority in being only woman and almost an offense in being treated only as woman. From this has arisen a false vocation: she has sought to take revenge, to claim her dignity, to show her value, by measuring herself against the man. The result is the mixed being, deprived of relation to the forces of its deepest nature. As in magnetism, the creative spark is higher and more alive the more decided the polarity, the more the man is truly man and the woman truly woman. When the absolute man and the absolute woman are lost, the higher dimensions of sex that Tradition recognized in multiple forms are lost with them, and the regime of sexual unions, the possibilities of transfiguration, and even the sense of procreation are correspondingly reduced.

The demand the chapter places on the reader is therefore exact. Man and woman are two types whose realization requires the overcoming of mixture. The masculine principle remains principle in itself; the feminine principle finds its limit and its transfiguration in orientation toward that stability. The paths of absolute affirmation and of absolute dedication are distinct; both can be luminous when lived with purity. To recover the condition under which each sex tends toward its absolute form, under which the woman participates mediately in the sacred order, and under which the polarity remains decided, is to recover the experiential possibilities that the modern leveling of the sexes has closed. The recognition that the modern demand for equality of the sexes is itself a symptom of the same process that has dissolved hierarchical form is the condition for standing against the reduction of man and woman to the quantitative and the indefinite.
