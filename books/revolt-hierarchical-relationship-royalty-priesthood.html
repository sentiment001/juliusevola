On the Hierarchical Relationship Between Royalty and Priesthood

“Regality enjoys primacy over the priesthood, just as, symbolically speaking, the sun has primacy over the moon and the man over the woman. In a certain sense this is the same primacy over Abraham’s priesthood that was traditionally attributed to the priestly regality of Melchizedek, who performed sacrifices in the name of the Almighty, the God of Victory.” Revolt Against the Modern World

The original synthesis of the two powers is reestablished in the person of the consecrated king. The hierarchical relationships that exist in every normal social order between royalty and the priestly caste remain clear. The priestly caste is merely the mediator of supernatural influences. Regality enjoys primacy over that mediation. The relation is the same as the relation of the sun to the moon and of the man to the woman. Melchizedek stands as the traditional figure of this primacy. He performed sacrifices in the name of the Almighty, the God of Victory, and held a rank above the priesthood of Abraham. Medieval defenders of the Ghibelline ideal returned to that figure when they asserted the supernatural dignity of the monarchy against the claims of the Church.

Even in civilizations that appear to be dominated by the priestly caste the correct relationship was preserved. Indo-Aryan texts state that the stock of the warrior deities arose from Brahman as a higher and more perfect form than Brahman itself. Nothing is greater than the warrior nobility. The priests themselves venerate the warrior when the consecration of the king occurs. The priestly caste stands in the relation of a mother or of a maternal matrix to the warrior caste. The regal type is the male principle. It surpasses, individuates, masters, and rules triumphantly over the spiritual force conceived as female. The hierarchy is not reversed by the fact that the priest performs the rite. The rite itself confirms the higher position of the one who receives it.

In the act of consecration the virile and warrior quality of the person to be initiated frees itself and rises to a higher plane. It then acts as an axis or as a pole of the sacred force. This is why the officiating priest must worship the king whom he consecrates. The king, according to the texts, still owes to the brahmana the respect owed to a mother. The relation remains hierarchical. Even the Manavadharmashastra, while upholding the primacy of the brahmana, compares the priest to water and to stone and the kshatriya to fire and to iron. Rulers do not prosper without priests and priests do not thrive without rulers. The priest is said to be the root of the law. The ruler is the peak. The root supports. The peak stands above.

These ideas were not alien to early Christianity. In certain periods the pope prostrated himself before the emperor. The emperor presided over ecclesiastical councils. The claim that the one who blesses is superior to the one who is blessed, and that the king must therefore obey the priest, is a later pretension. In Europe that pretension took form in the Investiture Controversy. When the priesthood demands recognition of its hierarchical superiority over royalty and requires obedience, the traditional truth is inverted. The inversion is heresy in the precise sense of the word: a departure from the right measure of the order. The sacred king belongs to the same stock as the gods. He ignores the feeling of religious subordination. He cannot accept a claim to supremacy advanced by those whose function is mediation.

The title pontifex maximus was originally a function of the king and of the Roman Augustus. Its assumption by the head of the Christian religion is a usurpation. The symbols and the powers that later gathered around the papacy had belonged to the regal function. The conflict that arises is not a conflict between a spiritual authority and a merely temporal power. It is a conflict between two forms of authority that are both spiritual, yet irreducible. The royal form is the solar form. It masters the force. The priestly form is the lunar form. It mediates the force. When the lunar form claims the place of the solar form, the order is already broken. The Ghibelline defense of the Empire preserved the older hierarchy. Later ages retained the titles and lost the hierarchy.

The demand the chapter places on the reader is therefore exact. Authority that places the mediator above the one who stands as the axis of the force has already abandoned the traditional measure. The inversion appears first as a spiritual pretension. It later appears as a political conflict. In both cases the result is the same: the higher principle is subordinated to the lower. The recognition of the original hierarchy is the condition for seeing why the medieval Empire and the Ghibelline ideal still carried a residual force that later forms of authority no longer possess.
