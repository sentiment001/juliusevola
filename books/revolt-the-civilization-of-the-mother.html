The Civilization of the Mother

“The Demetrian spirituality, pure and calm like the lunar light, defines typologically the Silver Age and, in all probability, the cycle of the first Atlantic civilization. Historically, however, it has nothing primordial. It is already a product of transformation.” Revolt Against the Modern World

The civilization of the Mother is not without relation to totemism in the most general sense. It is already significant that in the Hindu tradition the way of the ancestors (pitr-yana), opposed to the solar way of the gods, appears as synonymous with the way of the Mother. Everywhere in the South, from Mesopotamia to the Atlantic, one finds Neolithic statuettes of the Mother with the Son. In Crete the land of origin was called, instead of “patrie,” “land of the mother” (metris), a particularity that relates this civilization in a specific way to the Atlantic-southern civilization and to the substratum of still more ancient cults of the South. The polarity can be expressed by the oppositions of civilizations of the heroes and civilizations of the Mothers, solar idea and chthonic-lunar idea, paternal right and matriarchy, aristocratic ethic of difference and orgiastic-communist promiscuity, Olympian ideal of the supraworld and pantheist mysticism, positive right of the Imperium and natural right. This Mother is sometimes the earth, or sometimes the law of nature, conceived of as a force by which the gods themselves are compelled. Among her other aspects there are various differentiations: she is both Demeter as goddess of agriculture and organized earth and Aphrodite-Astarte as principle of orgiastic ecstasies, Dionysian wantonnesses, hetaeric dissoluteness, whose analogic correspondence is the wild swamp or forest.

The opposition between the Demetrian type and the Aphrodisian type corresponds to the opposition between the pure, transformed form and the inferior, grossly telluric form of the cult of the Mother, which reappears in the last stages of decomposition and sensualization of the civilization of the Silver Age. It is the Demetrian spirituality, pure and calm like the lunar light, that defines typologically the Silver Age and, in all probability, the cycle of the first Atlantic civilization. Historically, however, it has nothing primordial. It is already a product of transformation. On the other hand, the so-called natural right, the communist promiscuity proper to many savage societies, especially of the South (Africa, Polynesia) and even the Slavic mir, almost always brings one back to the characteristic framework of the civilization of the Mother, even where there was no matriarchy and where it is less a matter of mistovariations of the primordial Boreal civilization than of residues of the tellurism inherent in inferior autochthonous races. Bachofen noted that against the substratum of a more ancient world, suffused with a civilization of the Mother, the opposite civilization, virile and paternal, developed to supplant and defeat it, even though, at a later point, at the closing of a cycle, at least in some countries, it was swept away again.

In Rome the introduction of Demeter, Dionysus, and Kore by the Sibylline Books in 258 marks the first great phase of the spiritual offensive. It conducted the two great terrestrial goddesses of nature with their orgiastic companion, symbol of every confusion and anti-virile mysticism, into the world that early Rome had built through its destruction by arms of races and power centers which themselves had already incarnated finished, spiritually infused forms. In 249 Dis Pater and Proserpina, the nether-telluric divinities, the most typical personifications of that which opposes Olympic and Apollonian ideals, entered Rome. These were followed in 217 by an Aphroditic divinity, Venus Erycina, and finally in 205, in the most critical moment of the Punic Wars, Cybele, the Magna Mater, the Matriarch of this entire cycle, she who could call herself the personification of the entire Pelasgian-Asiatic and pre-Roman spirit. The man prayed, invoked, and sacrificed on his feet. This is already an index of a profound transformation, of the passage from one mentality to another. The civilization of the Mother therefore represents the lunar and Demetrian conditioning that marks the Silver Age, the first major differentiation of the original Uranian and polar orientation under the influence of the Southern Light and the telluric principle.

The demand the chapter places on the reader is therefore exact. The civilization of the Mother is not an original state. It is a product of transformation, the lunar and Demetrian form of the Silver Age in which the pure solar principle of the North has already been conditioned by the generative and telluric element. Demeter and Cybele, the pure lunar light and the Magna Mater, mark this intermediate condition against which the later heroic and Uranian restorations must be measured. To recognize it is to hold the distinction between the solar and paternal against the lunar and maternal, and to refuse the myth that makes the Mother the primordial source. Without that recognition the cycles of decadence remain unanchored, and the revolt against the modern world loses the precise measure of the first major intrusion of the Southern Light.
