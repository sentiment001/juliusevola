The Regression of the Castes

“A progressive shift of power and type of civilization has occurred from one caste to the next since prehistoric times (from sacred leaders, to a warrior aristocracy, to the merchants, and finally, to the serfs); these castes in traditional civilizations corresponded to the qualitative differentiation of the main human possibilities.” Revolt Against the Modern World

The preceding chapters have supplied the elements needed to state an objective law governing the successive stages of decadence: the law of the regression of the castes. From prehistoric times power and the type of civilization have descended from one caste to the next. The sequence runs from the sacred leaders, through the warrior aristocracy, to the merchants, and finally to the serfs. In traditional civilizations these four castes corresponded to a qualitative differentiation of the principal human possibilities. Against this general movement the conflicts of peoples, the life of nations, and the accidents of ordinary history play only a secondary and contingent role.

The age of the first caste belongs to a remote and almost mythical past. In the West the representatives of divine royalty and the leaders who embodied both spiritual and temporal power, the figures of spiritual virility and Olympian sovereignty, stand at a great distance. Through the gradual deterioration of the Light of the North the process of decadence has unfolded. The Ghibelline ideal of the Holy Roman Empire marks the last echo of that highest tradition.

Once the apex disappeared, authority descended to the level immediately below: the caste of the warriors. Monarchs appeared who were only military leaders, lords of temporal justice, and later politically absolute sovereigns. Regality of blood replaced regality of the spirit. In a few cases the formula of divine right survived, but it was empty of real content. In the West, with the dissolution of the medieval ecumene, the passage into this second phase became complete and definitive. The bond that held the state together no longer had a religious character; it became a warrior bond of loyalty, faithfulness, and honor. This was the age and the cycle of the great European monarchies.

A second collapse followed. The aristocracies decayed and the monarchies shook. Through revolutions and constitutions they were reduced to useless institutions subject to the “will of the nation,” or they were simply ousted. The principle that characterized this state was “The king reigns but he does not rule.” Parliamentary republics and the formation of capitalist oligarchies revealed the shift of power from the second caste to the modern equivalent of the third: the mercantile class. The kings of coal, oil, and iron replaced the earlier kings of blood and of spirit. Antiquity had already known sporadic anticipations of this phenomenon. In Rome and in Greece the aristocracy of wealth repeatedly forced the hierarchical structure, pursued high office, undermined sacred laws, and infiltrated the militia, the priesthood, and the consulship. In the Middle Ages the rebellion of the communes and the rise of mercantile formations prepared the way. The solemn proclamation of the rights of the Third Estate in France marked the decisive stage, followed by the varieties of bourgeois revolution that employed liberal and democratic ideologies for their own ends. The social bond itself changed character. It was no longer a warrior fides of faithfulness and honor; it became a utilitarian and economic agreement based on personal convenience and material interest, the kind of contract only a merchant could conceive. Gold became a powerful instrument. Those who knew how to acquire and multiply it—capitalism, high finance, industrial trusts—virtually controlled political power and the instruments of opinion, behind the appearances of democracy. Aristocracy gave way to plutocracy; the warrior gave way to the banker and the industrialist. The economy triumphed on every front. Trafficking with money and charging interest, activities once confined to the ghettos, invaded the new civilization. In the promised land of Protestant puritanism, Americanism, capitalism, and the distilled Jewish spirit coexisted. The modern representatives of secularized Judaism found the ways to world domination open before them. Marx himself stated the principle: the mundane principles of Judaism are practical necessity and the pursuit of advantage; its earthly god is money; through the Jew money became a world power and the practical Jewish spirit became the spirit of the Christian peoples. The Jews emancipated themselves insofar as the Christians became Jews. The codification of traffic with gold as interest-bearing loan became the foundation of the aberrant development of banking, high finance, and pure economy that spread like a cancer through the modern world. This is the fundamental time of the age of the merchants.

Finally the crisis of bourgeois society, class struggle, the proletarian revolt against capitalism, the manifesto of the Third International, and the organization of the masses into the cadres of a socialist civilization of labor marked the third collapse. Power tended to pass into the hands of the lowest of the traditional castes: the caste of the beasts of burden and the standardized individuals. The result was a reduction of every horizon and every value to the plane of matter, the machine, and the reign of quantity. The Russian Revolution was the prelude. The new ideal became the proletarian ideal of a universal and communist civilization.

The awakening and gushing forth of elemental subhuman forces within the structures of the modern world may be compared to a man who can no longer endure the tension of the spirit (first caste), and eventually not even the tension of the will as a free force that animates the body (warrior caste), and who therefore gives way to the subpersonal forces of the organic system and reacts almost magnetically under the impulse of another life that replaces his own. The ideas and passions of the demos escape men’s control and begin to act as if they possessed an autonomous and dreadful life of their own. They pit nations and collectivities against each other and produce unprecedented conflicts and crises. At the end of the process, once the total collapse has occurred, there awaits an international system under the brutal symbols of the hammer and the sickle.

These are the horizons facing the contemporary world. Only by adhering to free activity can a man be truly free and realize himself. When he concentrates on practical and utilitarian goals, on economic achievements, and on whatever once belonged exclusively to the inferior castes, he abdicates, disintegrates, loses his center, and opens himself to infernal forces of which he becomes the unwilling and unconscious instrument. Contemporary society resembles an organism that has shifted from a human to a subhuman type, in which every activity and reaction is determined by the needs and dictates of purely physical life. The dominating principles are those that belonged to the material part of the traditional hierarchies: gold and work. These two elements, almost without exception, affect every possibility of existence and shape the ideologies and myths that testify to the gravity of the modern perversion of all values.

The quadripartite regression is not limited to the sociopolitical domain; it invests every domain of civilization. In architecture the dominant building passes from the temple (first caste) to the fortress and castle (warriors), to the city-state enclosed by walls (merchants), to the factory, and finally to the rational and dull buildings that are the hives of mass-man. The family, which at the origin possessed a sacred foundation, shifted to an authoritarian model (patria potestas in a merely juridical sense), then to a bourgeois and conventional one, and tends toward dissolution when the party, the people, and society supersede it in importance and dignity. The notion of war followed the same descent: from the doctrine of the sacred war and of the mors triumphalis, to war waged in the name of the right and honor of one’s lord (warrior caste), to conflicts determined by national ambitions contingent upon the plans and interests of a supremacist economy and industry (merchants), and finally to the communist theory according to which war among nations is a bourgeois residue and the only just war is the world revolution of the proletarian class against the capitalist and so-called imperialist world (serfs). In the aesthetic domain the movement ran from a symbolic and sacred art closely bound to the possibilities of prediction and magic (first caste), to the predominance of epic art and poems (warriors), to a romantic, conventional, sentimentalist, erotic, and psychological art produced for bourgeois consumption, and finally to “social” or “socially involved” views that advocate an art for the use and consumption of the masses. The traditional world knew the superindividual unity of the orders: first the ascetic and monastic orders, then the knightly orders (warriors), then the unity sworn in Masonic lodges that prepared the revolution of the Third Estate and the advent of democracy, and finally the network of revolutionary and activist cadres of the Communist International bent on the destruction of the previous order.

It is on the plane of ethics that the degradation is particularly visible. The first age was characterized by the ideal of spiritual virility, initiation, and an ethics aimed at overcoming all human bonds. The age of the warriors was characterized by the ideal of heroism, victory, and lordship, and by the aristocratic ethics of honor, faithfulness, and chivalry. In the age of the merchants the predominant ideals became pure economics, profit, prosperity, and science as an instrument of technical and industrial progress that propels production and new profits in a consumer society. The advent of the serfs corresponds to the elevation of the slave’s principle—work—to the status of a religion. The hatred of the slave proclaims sadistically: “If anyone will not work, neither let him eat.” The slave’s self-congratulating stupidity creates sacred incense from the exhalations of human sweat: “Work ennobles man,” “the religion of work,” “work as a social and ethical duty.” The ancient world despised work only because it knew action. The opposition between action and work—between the spiritual, pure, and free pole and the material, impure pole impregnated only with human possibilities—was the basis of that contempt. The loss of the sense of this opposition, and the animal-like subordination of the former to the latter, characterizes the last ages. Where in antiquity every work, through an inner transfiguration owing to its purity and its meaning as an offering oriented upward, could redeem itself until it became a symbol of action, now, following an upheaval in the opposite direction, every residue of action tends to be degraded to the form of work. The degeneration of the ancient aristocratic and sacred ethics into the modern plebeian and materialistic morality is expressively characterized by this shift from the plane of action to the plane of work. Superior men who lived in a not distant past either acted or directed actions. Modern man works. The only real difference today is that which exists between the various kinds of work: there are “intellectual” workers and those who use their limbs and machines. The notion of action is dying out, together with that of absolute personality. Among all the commissioned arts, antiquity regarded as most disgraceful those devoted to the pursuit of pleasure; that is precisely the kind of work most respected in the present age. Beginning with the scientist, the technician, and the politician, and with the rationalized system of productive organization, work is supposed to lead to the realization of an ideal more fitting for a human animal: an easier life that is more enjoyable and safer, with the maximization of well-being and physical comfort. The contemporary breed of artists and “creative minds” of the bourgeoisie is the equivalent of that class of luxury servants that catered to the pleasure and distractions of the Roman patriciate and later of the medieval feudal lords.

The same themes appear on the ideal and speculative plane. During the age of humanism the antitraditional and plebeian theme emerged in Giordano Bruno, who inverted traditional values and extolled the age of human effort and work against the Golden Age (of which he knew nothing) in a masochistic fashion. Bruno called divine the brutish drive of human need, because that drive produces increasingly wonderful arts and inventions, removes mankind further from a Golden Age he regarded as animalistic and lazy, and draws human beings closer to God. In this we find an anticipation of the ideologies connected to the age of the French Revolution that made work the main element of the social myth and revived the messianic theme in terms of work and machines while singing the praises of progress. Modern man began to apply to the universe and to project on an ideal plane the experiences he had nurtured in the workshops and factories, experiences by which the soul itself became a product. Bergson, who exalted the élan vital, drew the analogy, as only a modern could, between technical productive activity inspired by a mere practical principle and the ways of intelligence itself. Having covered with ridicule the ancient inert ideal of knowledge as contemplation, the entire effort of modern epistemology in its most radical trajectories consists in assimilating knowledge to productive work according to the postulates “To know is to do” and “One can only really know what one does.” Verum et factum convertuntur. And since, according to the unrealism of these currents, to be means to know, the spirit is identified with the idea, and the productive and immanent knowing process is identified with the process of reality, the way of the fourth caste is reflected in the highest regions and posits itself as their foundational truth. There is an activism on the plane of philosophical theories that agrees with the world created by the advent of the last caste and its civilization of work.

This advent is reflected in the modern ideologies of progress and evolution, which have distorted with scientific irresponsibility any superior vision of history, promoted the definitive abandonment of traditional truths, and created the most specious alibis for the justification and glorification of modern man. The myth of evolutionism is nothing other than the profession of faith of the upstart. If in recent times the West no longer believes in a transcendent origin but in an origin from below; if it no longer believes in the nobility of the origins but in the notion that civilization arises out of barbarism, religion from superstition, man from animal (Darwin), thought from matter, and every spiritual form from the sublimation or transposition of the stuff that originates instinct, libido, and the complexes of the collective unconscious (Freud, Jung)—we see in all this not so much the result of a deviated quest as an alibi, something that a civilization created by lower beings and by the revolution of the serfs and pariahs against the ancient aristocratic society necessarily had to believe in and wish to be true. There is not a dimension in which, in one form or another, the evolutionary myth has not succeeded in infiltrating with destructive consequences. The results have been the overthrow of every value, the suppression of all sense of truth, and the elaboration and connection (as in an unbreakable magical circle) of the world inhabited by a deconsecrated and deluded mankind. In agreement with historicism, so-called post-Hegelian Idealism came to identify the essence of the Absolute Spirit with its becoming and its self-creation. This Spirit was no longer conceived as a Being that is, that dominates, and that possesses itself; the self-made man has almost become the new metaphysical model.

It is not easy to separate the process of regression along the way of gold (age of merchants) from the regression along the way of work (age of serfs), since these ways are interdependent. For all practical purposes, just as work as a universal duty is no longer perceived as a repugnant, absurd, and unnatural value, so to be paid does not seem repugnant but on the contrary seems very natural. Money, which no longer burns the hands it touches, has established an invisible bond of slavery that is worse and more depraved than that which the high spiritual stature of lords and conquerors used to retain and justify. Just as any form of action tends to become yet another form of work, so is it always associated with payment. While on the one hand action reduced to work is judged by its efficiency, and man is valued by his practical success and by his profit, and while Calvin acted as a pimp by seeing that profit and wealth were shrouded in the mysticism of a divine election, on the other hand the specter of hunger and unemployment lurks upon these new slaves as a more fearful threat than the threat of the whip in ancient times. It is possible to distinguish a general phase in which the yearning for profit displayed by single individuals who pursue wealth and power is the central motif (the phase corresponding to the advent of the third caste) from a further phase that is still unfolding, characterized by a sovereign economy that has become almost independent or collectivized (the advent of the last caste).

The law of the regression of the castes therefore supplies the objective measure of the entire historical process that has brought the West to its present condition. The same law is reflected in every domain: in architecture, in the family, in the idea of war, in art, in the form of collective organizations, and above all in ethics and in the speculative order. Gold and work stand as the last principles. The man who accepts them as the measure of existence has already abdicated the higher possibilities that once defined the human type capable of standing upright under a superior measure. The cycle closes under the sign of the mass and of the machine.
