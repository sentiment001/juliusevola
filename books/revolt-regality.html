Regality

“Pontifex means ‘builder of bridges,’ or of ‘paths’ (pons, in ancient times, also meant ‘path’) connecting the natural and the supernatural dimensions.” — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

The regal function is the highest visible form of the higher nature in the order of the world. Traditional civilizations did not treat supreme authority as a political matter. They ignored the idea that the roots of power lie in force, violence, or ordinary human qualities such as intelligence, wisdom, or physical courage. The foundation of the king’s authority was his transcendent and non-human quality. This quality was not an opinion or a title. It was a real presence that those around him recognized and that ordered their lives. The king stood as the concrete point where the higher principle entered the lower world. Without that presence the lower world has no true center and therefore no true order.

The word pontifex names the function. It means builder of bridges or of paths that connect the natural order to the supernatural order. The king is that bridge. He does not stand for the people in the modern sense. He stands above them as the living path by which the higher influences reach the human domain. His person is the axis. When he holds his place the forces of the higher nature move through the structures of the society. When that place is vacant or occupied by a man who lacks the quality, the structures remain but the orientation is lost. The people then live under forms that no longer carry the higher measure. The king’s accountability is not to numbers or to consent. It is to the higher order itself. The traditional view holds that the king answers to the divine principle and to the natural law that flows from it. Modern ideas reverse this and make the ruler answer to the lower element.

Solar symbolism marks the character of true regality. The sun is fixed, luminous, and generative. It overcomes darkness by its own nature. The traditional king is solar in the same sense. He has overcome the lower nature in himself and therefore can serve as the fixed point for others. Lunar or merely priestly elements are subordinate. They reflect or mediate; they do not originate the order. The original hierarchy places the regal function above the priestly. The king is not made sacred by a rite performed by priests. The rite confirms and transmits a quality that is already present. Solar kingship appears across traditional civilizations as the visible sign that authority comes from above and remains oriented upward. The king is not a manager of interests. He is the living symbol and the living force of the higher principle in the human world.

Modern conceptions of authority reverse every point. They locate the source of power in the will of the many, in contract, in force, or in technical competence. Each of these is a lower principle. None of them can supply the bridge. A ruler who holds office by the consent of the lower element or by the management of material forces does not occupy the regal place. His authority is therefore fraudulent in the traditional sense. It may organize men for a time. It cannot orient them toward the higher nature. The traditional world knew that any law, any institution, and any command is empty unless it is derived from the superior principle of Being and oriented upward. Regality is the concrete form of that derivation in the political and social order. Without it the structures of a civilization remain closed in the domain of becoming.

The demand the chapter places on the reader is the recognition that authority is either from above or it is not authority. The modern alternatives (numbers, force, skill, or consent) are all variants of the lower nature. They can produce administration. They cannot produce order. The traditional king is not an archaic political form. He is the living proof that a man can stand as the bridge between the two natures and thereby give the lower world a true center. To hold this idea is to measure every claim to rule by whether it carries the higher quality or remains closed in the lower. The absence of that quality is the decisive fact of the age, not a secondary defect of its institutions.
