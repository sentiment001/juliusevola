The Mystery of the Rite

“In the traditional world every act, individual or collective, carried a determined ritual element that served as its support, its guide from above, and its transfiguring force. Establishing the rites was counted among the first duties of imperial government, for the rites are the channels through which the ways of Heaven are reached.” — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

The divine-right king stands as the center of the traditional state. What binds the particular parts and activities of that state to the center, and what allows individuals to participate in the transcendent influence belonging to the chief, is a double element: the rite and fidelity. The rite is not an accessory ceremony added to ordinary action. It is the concrete means by which the higher principle enters the lower order and orients it. Without the rite the structures of law, state, and empire remain closed in the domain of becoming. With the rite those structures become channels. The classical world could say that the priest of the sacred fire, by the performance of his rite day after day, saved the city. The fire is kept. The contact is maintained. The higher influences continue to reach the human domain.

Establishing the rites is the first among the duties considered of greatest importance for the government of an empire. The rites are the channels through which the ways of Heaven can be reached. In the Hindu tradition the places of sacrifice are regarded as the very seats of rta, the order that is simultaneously truth, reality, and law. The same root appears in the Latin ritus. The sacrificial place is not a stage for symbolic drama. It is the point at which the higher measure is applied to the lower forces and those forces are subjected and ordered. Every significant act of traditional life, whether private or public, carried a determined ritual element. That element served as its support, as the guide coming from above, and as the force that transfigures the act from a merely human performance into a participation in the higher nature. The tradition of the rites and of the sacrifices, like the tradition of the laws themselves (often indistinguishable from them under the name of sacred law), referred in both the private and the public order to a non-human being or to one who had become non-human.

All of this is unknown territory for the modern secular mind. For that mind the rites, even when they are not dismissed as outdated superstition, count only as simple ceremonies. At most they may be granted a symbolic, aesthetic, or emotional value. The operative efficacy is denied. The modern view begins from the restriction of experience to the physical and therefore cannot recognize a channel that transmits influences from a dimension prior to and beyond the physical. Once the efficacy is denied, the rite collapses into empty form. What remains is either pure horizontal action or a psychological substitute that produces feeling without contact. Traditional man knew that the rite works according to an objective law of correspondences. The higher principle is not imagined into existence by the participant. It is reached through the correct performance of the act that corresponds to it. The mystery of the rite is precisely this operative power: the capacity of a determined action, performed under the higher measure, to reestablish the contact that ordinary becoming severs.

Sacrificial rites occupy a central place in this order. Sacrifice is not the destruction of value. It is the conscious offering of a lower element so that a higher element may take its place. The lower force is subjected, transformed, and raised. In the heroic form of sacrifice the man offers his own lower nature. The act produces a qualitative change: the participant moves from the condition of the one who is carried by becoming to the condition of the one who stands in relation to being. Initiation and the rite are closely bound. Not every man is qualified to perform every rite. The higher rites require a prior transformation of the operator. When the priestly function falls or becomes merely formal, the channels close. The rites continue as external forms but lose the power to transmit. The traditional world treated this loss as a grave regression. It knew that the maintenance of the divine connection depends on the integrity of those who perform the rites and on the fidelity of those who receive their effects.

The demand the chapter places on the reader is the recognition that action is either oriented by the rite or it remains closed in the lower nature. The modern alternatives (pure technique, pure will, pure utility) can organize force and produce results inside the domain of becoming. They cannot reestablish the contact with the higher principle. The rite is not an archaic addition to life. It is the concrete proof that a determined action can serve as the channel by which the ways of Heaven reach the human order. To hold this idea is to measure every significant act, private or public, by whether it carries the ritual element that supports it from above or remains a merely horizontal performance. The absence of that element is the decisive fact of the age. Without it the structures of law, state, and empire lose their living connection to the center, and ordinary action remains subject to the stream of becoming.
