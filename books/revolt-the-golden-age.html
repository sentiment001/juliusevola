The Golden Age

“Cycle of Being, solar cycle, cycle of light as glory, cycle of the living in an eminent and transcendent sense—these are the characteristics of the first age, the Golden Age, or the ‘era of the gods’ found in the traditional memories.” Revolt Against the Modern World

The Golden Age is not a poetic dream of ease or a later invention of nostalgia. It is the morphological type of an original civilization that stood naturally and totally in conformity with the traditional spirit. In that first era the highest function of regality appears with its proper symbols: polarity, solarity, height, stability, glory, and life taken in a higher sense. These attributes mark the presence of a principle that does not derive from the contingent or the merely human. Gold itself, in the traditional view, does not first signify material wealth. It designates the incorruptible, the solar, the luminous, and the bright. In the Hellenic memory anything radiant, sacred, and great could be called golden. Later mixed traditions still retain traces of this state in their ruling elites, who appear as those who still enjoy or reproduce the being of the origins. The age is therefore the measure against which every subsequent deviation is judged.

The first era is essentially the era of Being, and therefore of truth in a transcendent sense. The Hindu designation Satya Yuga makes this plain: sat means being, so satya is truth as that which is. The Latin name Saturn, king or god of the Golden Age, carries the same reference. Saturn corresponds to the Hellenic Kronos. In the name one finds the Aryan root sat, being, joined to an attributive ending. The era of Being is also the era of spiritual stability. Representations of the primordial site in which this cycle unfolded show the symbols of terra firma surrounded by waters, of the island, of the mountain, or of the middle land. These are not mere geography. They are the fixed center that does not drift with the contingent. From this center the qualities that characterize every caste in the first age follow: purity of heart, justice, wisdom, and adherence to sacred institutions. The form is not imposed from outside. It is the natural expression of a state that has not yet fallen into mixture.

As the age of Being the first era is also the era of the Living in the eminent sense. According to Hesiod, ordinary death, the end that bequeaths Hades for most people, appears only in the last two ages. During the Golden Age of Kronos mortal people lived as if they were gods. They did not know the separation that later makes death an absolute terminus. The cycle is therefore solar: a cycle of light as glory and of life that is not merely biological duration. Traditional memories call it the era of the gods. The polar symbols of height and stability belong to this living. The mountain and the middle land are the seats from which such life radiates. What later ages experience as loss is here still present as the ordinary condition of the higher type. The living are those who participate in the principle rather than those who merely endure the passage of time.

The morphological assessment of the Golden Age therefore supplies the ideal type against which the later cycles are measured. It is not yet the place of historical and geographical trajectories, which follow in the subsequent treatment of the pole and the Hyperborean region. It is the pure form: an original civilization in which the traditional spirit is not a residual or a restored element but the natural and total condition. Symbols of regality, the era of Being and truth, the living in the transcendent sense, and the fixed center all converge on one point. Every later age is a further conditioning of this state by the human, mortal, and contingent element. The demand the chapter places on the reader is therefore exact. To recognize the Golden Age is to hold the higher measure that the doctrine of the four ages requires. Without that measure the involution remains abstract, and the revolt against the modern world has no original standard by which to judge the present darkening.
