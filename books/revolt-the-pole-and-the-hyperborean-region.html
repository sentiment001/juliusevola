The Pole and the Hyperborean Region

“According to the tradition, in an epoch of high prehistory that corresponds to the Golden Age or the age of Being, the symbolic ‘polar’ island or land would have been a real region situated in the far North, near the place where the Arctic pole of the earth is found today.” Revolt Against the Modern World

A particular attribute of the primordial age allows precise historical and geographical representations to be joined to it. The symbolism of the pole has already been noted: the island or firm land that stands for spiritual stability against the contingency of the waters, and that therefore serves as the residence of transcendent men, heroes, and immortals. The mountain, the altitude, or the supreme region, with the Olympian meanings attached to them, joined the same polar symbolism in the ancient traditions. The pole is applied to the supreme center of the world and therefore also to the archetype of every domination in the higher sense. This is the precise point at which one can insert oneself into the events conditioned by time. In an epoch of high prehistory that corresponds to the Golden Age or the age of Being, the symbolic polar island or land would have been a real region situated in the far North, near the place where the Arctic pole of the earth is found today. That region was inhabited by beings who, in possession of a non-human spirituality (corresponding to the notions of gold, glory, light, and life already indicated), later evoked by the symbolism suggested by their very location, constituted the race that possessed the Uranian tradition in the pure and unique state. It was the central and most direct source of the varied forms and expressions this tradition assumed among other races and civilizations.

The memory of this Arctic seat is the patrimony of the traditions of many peoples, either in the form of real geographic allusions or of symbols of its function and original significance, often transferred to a super-historical significance or applied to other centers that may be considered copies of the original one. Above all one notices the interplay of the Arctic theme with the Atlantic theme, of the mystery of the North with the mystery of the West, because the principal seat that succeeded the original and traditional pole was actually situated in the Atlantic. Among the many designations of the Hyperborean center that came to be applied also to the Atlantic center was Thule, or the White Island, or Island of Splendor (the Hindu Sveta-dvipa; the Hellenic Leuke island; the original seed of the Aryan race or Ariyana Vaego in ancient Iran); and Land of the Sun, or Land of Apollo, that is, Avalon. Concordant memories in all Indo-European traditions speak of the disappearance of such a seat (which later on was mythologized) following an ice age or a flood. This is the real historical counterpart of the various allusions to something that, beginning with a given period, has allegedly been lost or become hidden and untraceable. After having been the lord of this earth, the king of the Golden Age, Kronos-Saturn, was dethroned and castrated (that is, deprived of the power to beget, to give life to a new stock); he still lives, though asleep, in a region located in the far North, close to the Arctic sea, which was also called the Cronid Sea.

In the doctrine of the dvipa one finds significant references to the Arctic center, although mixed with other elements. The already mentioned Sveta-dvipa, or Island of Splendor, is located in the extreme North, and one often speaks of the Uttarakuru as a race originating from the North. Both the Sveta-dvipa and the Kuru form part of the Jambu-dvipa, that is, of the polar insular continent which is the first of the various dvipa and at the same time their common center. Its memory mixes with that of the Saka-dvipa, situated in the white sea or sea of milk, that is, in the Arctic sea. In this place no deviation from the law from above occurred. The Thule of the Greeks, according to a widely diffused idea, had the character of Land of the Sun. Someone said: Thule ultima a sole nomen habens. Though the etymology is obscure and uncertain, it is no less significant of the idea the ancients had of that divine region and corresponds to the same solar character possessed by the ancient Tlappallan, Tullan or Tulla (contraction of Tonalan, the place of the Sun), original homeland of the Toltecs and paradise of their heroes. It likewise points back to the country of the Hyperboreans, since according to the sacred geography of ancient traditions the Hyperboreans were a mysterious race that lived in eternal light and whose country would have been the residence and the fatherland of the Delphic Apollo, the Dorian god of light, the Pure, the Resplendent, also represented as a god of gold and a god of the Golden Age. Thule is therefore confused both with the legendary Hyperborean homeland situated in the far North, from which the original Achaean stocks brought the Delphic Apollo, and with the isle of Ogygia, the navel of the sea, which lies far away on the vast ocean and which Plutarch situates north of Britain, near the Arctic place where Kronos, the king of the Golden Age, still remains plunged in sleep, where the sun disappears for only one hour a day during an entire month and where the darkness during that single hour is not thick.

As far as the emigration of the Boreal race is concerned, two distinct major streams issued forth, one from north to south, and the other, later, from west to east. Carrying everywhere the same spirit, the same blood, the same body of symbols, signs, and words, groups of Hyperboreans first reached North America and the northern regions of the Eurasian continent. After several tens of thousands of years a second great wave of emigration appears to have advanced as far as Central America but concentrated above all in a region, today disappeared, situated in the Atlantic area, where it would have constituted a center in the image of the polar center, corresponding to the Atlantis of the accounts of Plato and Diodorus. Originally the Atlantic center was supposed to reproduce the polar function of the Hyperborean seat, and this circumstance is the source of frequent interferences in the matter of traditions and memories. These interferences should not prevent one from detecting, in a later period still belonging to the highest prehistory, a transformation of civilization and of spirituality, a differentiation that marks the passage from the first era to the second (from the Golden Age to the Silver Age) and opens the way to the third era, the Bronze Age or Titanic Age, which one could even, strictly speaking, qualify as Atlantean. The legend of Atlas holding the world on his shoulders, while under one aspect corresponding to the punishment of the Titan Atlas who according to some participated in the struggle against the Olympians, under another aspect may serve to symbolize the polar regency itself, the function of pole, of support or spiritual axis that, after the Hyperboreans, the Atlantic people in a first period would have assumed. The Tuatha de Danann, the divine race that came to Ireland from the western island of Avalon, guided by Ogma Grian-aineach, the hero of sunny countenance, correspond to this, as does the white and solar Quetzalcoatl who came with his companions from the land situated beyond the waters to America.

The demand the chapter places on the reader is therefore exact. The Golden Age is not an abstract morphological type alone. It had a real seat in the far North, the Hyperborean region whose memory survives as Thule, the White Island of Splendor, Avalon, the Land of the Sun, and the sleeping Kronos near the Arctic sea. From that polar center two great streams of emigration carried the same Uranian tradition southward and later westward, establishing an Atlantic replica that in time differentiated and prepared the later ages. To recognize the pole and the Hyperborean region is to hold the original geographical and spiritual center against which every subsequent mixture, darkening, and displacement is measured. Without that recognition the doctrine of the four ages remains floating, and the revolt against the modern world has no primordial seat from which to judge the present.
