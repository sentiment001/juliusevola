The Decline of Superior Races

“The modern world is far from being threatened by the danger of underpopulation; the cry of alarm some political leaders have launched in the past with the absurd slogan ‘there is power in numbers,’ is totally unfounded.” Revolt Against the Modern World

The modern world is far from being threatened by the danger of underpopulation. The cry of alarm some political leaders have launched in the past with the absurd slogan that there is power in numbers is totally unfounded. The truth is the opposite danger: the constant and untrammeled increase of population in purely quantitative terms. The deterioration of the population affects only those stocks that should be considered the bearers of the forces that preside over the demos and the world of the masses and that contribute to any authentic human greatness. When the racist worldview was criticized earlier it was noted that the occult power, when present, alive, and at work, constitutes the principle of a superior generation that reacts on the world of quantity by bestowing upon it a form and quality. In this regard one can say that the superior Western races have been agonizing for many centuries and that the increasing growth in world population has the same meaning as the swarming of worms on a decomposing organism or as the spreading of cancerous cells. Cancer is an uncontrolled hypertrophy of a plasma that devours the normal, differentiated structures of an organism after subtracting itself from the organism’s regulating laws.

This is the scenario facing the modern world: the regression and the decline of fecundating forces in the higher sense of the term and of the forces that bear forms parallels the unlimited proliferation of matter, of what is formless, of the masses. The superior races are dying out before the ineluctable logic of individualism, which especially in the so-called contemporary higher classes has caused people to lose all desire to procreate. Other degenerative factors connected to a mechanized and urbanized social life and especially to a civilization that no longer respects the healthy and creative limitations constituted by the castes and by the traditions of blood lineage accelerate the same process. The prolificity therefore concentrates in the lower social strata and in the inferior races, where the animal impulse is stronger than every calculation and every rational consideration. The inevitable effect is reverse selection: the ascent and the invasion of inferior elements against which the race of the higher classes and peoples, exhausted and defeated, can do little or nothing more as a spiritually dominating element.

If the expired civilization is propped up so as to make it look alive, and if men are treated like rabbits or stallions, their unions being carefully and rationally planned, no one should be fooled. What they will generate will either be a civilization of very beautiful animals destined to work, or, if the individualistic and utilitarian element predominates, a stronger law will lead the races toward the path of regression or extinction according to the same inexorability of the law of entropy and the degradation of energy. The feminization of spirituality is already preluded in ancient times. It determined the first alteration of the primordial tradition in the races where it prevailed. To follow this process of decadence together with all the others that have led to the collapse of the humanity of the origins is the object of the considerations that will be developed in the second part of the present work, through which the genesis and the face of the modern world will become manifest.

The demand the chapter places on the reader is therefore exact. Quantity without form is the signature of the age. The forces that once bestowed quality upon the world of the masses have regressed, and the proliferation of what is formless proceeds according to the logic of decomposition. Reverse selection elevates the inferior while the higher stocks, having lost contact with the principle that alone can confer stability and form, exhaust themselves. To recognize that the increase in numbers is not power but the symptom of a dying organism, and that any attempt to manage the process by calculation alone produces only work animals or further entropy, is to see the terrain on which the differentiated individual must stand. The recognition that the decline of superior races is the precondition of the modern world, not its accidental byproduct, is the condition for refusing both the quantitative illusion and the futile effort to prop up what has already lost its generating root from above.
