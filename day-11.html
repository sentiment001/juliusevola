Day 11 — The Need for a Center

"According to Tradition, every authority is fraudulent, every law is unjust and barbarous, every institution is vain and ephemeral unless they are ordered to the superior principle of Being." — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

A man without an inner center is carried by whatever current is strongest around him. The modern environment multiplies currents without pause: opinions, desires, group pressures, the constant demand to adjust. Most people learn to navigate by reading the surface of these forces. That is adaptation. It is also the slow erasure of any stable point from which judgment can be made.

Tradition speaks of a center that is not produced by personal preference and is not subject to the fluctuations of the age. This center is the presence of a higher principle that orders the rest of life. Without it, authority collapses into mere power, law into convenience, and institutions into temporary arrangements for material comfort. The modern world treats this absence as normal. It even calls it progress.

You will feel the friction immediately. The surrounding culture treats any claim of a superior principle as arrogance or superstition. That reaction is trained. Clear the space anyway. The work of orientation requires that you test whether your own life is ordered from something higher than mood and circumstance. If it is not, the rest of these readings will remain abstract.

A center does not announce itself with noise. It shows itself in the consistency of a man's decisions under pressure, in the things he refuses to trade, and in the quiet refusal to be fully absorbed by the horizontal plane. The next days will sharpen the diagnosis of the age. That diagnosis only becomes useful once a man has begun to sense the need for a point that does not move with the age.
