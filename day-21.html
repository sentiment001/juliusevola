Day 21 — Democracy and the Leveling Process

"Democracy is the regime of the quantity, of the mass, of the number. It is the political expression of the idea that the lower is equal to the higher, or rather that the lower has the right to judge and limit the higher." — Julius Evola, Men Among the Ruins

Democracy presents itself as the political form of freedom and fairness. In practice it is the institutionalization of the leveling process. Rank is treated as suspicion. Difference of type is treated as injustice. The numerical majority becomes the final measure of legitimacy. What can be counted outweighs what is superior in quality.

In a Traditional order the political form reflected qualitative hierarchy. Authority descended from a higher principle and was exercised by those capable of bearing it. The modern democratic principle reverses the direction. Power rises from below. The mass claims the right to decide the measure of the higher. The result is a systematic pressure against any form of life that cannot justify itself before the average.

You will meet this pressure daily. The demand to treat every opinion as equally weighty, every desire as equally valid, every type as interchangeable is the air of the democratic age. Resistance is labeled arrogance. Clear thinking requires that you test the claim. A society that systematically suppresses qualitative distinction does not produce equality of dignity. It produces a lowered standard that no one is allowed to rise above without apology.

The work here is not theatrical opposition to elections or parliaments. It is the recovery of an interior standard that does not submit to the numerical. A man who still measures by quality will find himself out of step with the official language of the age. That distance is the beginning of diagnosis. Without it the rest of the analysis remains theoretical.
