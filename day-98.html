Day 98 — Detachment as Strength

"Detachment is not weakness or withdrawal. In the conditions of a late age it is the form of strength that allows the man to remain upright while the current runs." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

The age confuses attachment with vitality. It treats the man who is deeply invested in the outcomes of the present order as the man of energy and commitment. The differentiated individual knows that attachment of that kind is a form of capture. The more the man identifies with the temporary forms of the age the more completely he is dissolved when those forms shift or collapse. Detachment is the refusal of that identification.

This detachment is active. It is the continuous exercise of the capacity to stand apart from the claims of the environment while still acting inside it. The man performs the required tasks. He masters the necessary instruments. He moves with the forces that must be moved with. Through all of it he remains the one who is not defined by the performance or by the forces. That remaining is the strength.

Without detachment the man is vulnerable to every acceleration and every reversal of the current. His mood, his sense of purpose, his very identity rise and fall with the surrounding conditions. With detachment he becomes capable of sustained presence across those changes. The presence is what the age lacks and what the higher type still requires.

The cultivation of detachment is therefore one of the central practical tasks of the phase. It is practiced in the refusal of certain pleasures of identification, in the acceptance of certain costs of non-belonging, in the steady return to the center when the pull of the collective becomes strong. The man who has formed this capacity can ride the tiger to the end of the phase and beyond it. The man who has not will be thrown long before the end.
