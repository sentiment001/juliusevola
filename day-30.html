Day 30 — The Break with the Past

"The modern world is defined by a deliberate rupture with the past. What was once regarded as a living inheritance to be continued and perfected is now treated as a dead weight to be discarded." — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

The break with the past is not merely a historical event. It is a continuous orientation. Each generation is taught that its task is to free itself from the forms, the authorities, and the standards of those who came before. Continuity is reinterpreted as stagnation. Inheritance is treated as limitation. The result is a culture that lives only in the present tense and that measures itself by the speed of its own self-cancellation.

Traditional civilizations understood themselves as links in a chain that stretched backward toward origins that were more than human. The past was not a museum. It was a living source of form and of legitimacy. When that relation is severed, the present becomes absolute and therefore empty. Nothing stands above the decisions of the moment.

Young men are formed inside this rupture. The official story of education is the story of progressive liberation from ancestral constraints. The cost is the loss of any standard that is not generated by the current generation itself. When that generation grows tired or confused, there is nothing left to fall back on.

Recovery does not mean the mechanical imitation of earlier styles. It means the recovery of the sense that one stands within a longer order and that one’s own formation is answerable to something that precedes the present age. That sense is the beginning of a real relation to the past.
