Day 338 — The Polar Stance

"The pole is the motionless point around which the world turns. In the man of Tradition that point is an interior reality." — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

The polar stance is the capacity to remain fixed at an inner axis while the surrounding world turns without a center. The traditional world knew the pole as both a cosmic symbol and a human possibility. The king, the rite, and the axis mundi made that possibility public. Those public poles have fallen. The stance remains possible in the man who refuses to spin with the field.

Young men who feel the need for an axis often look for residual poles outside themselves: movements, figures, and causes that promise to stop the turning. External poles in this age are usually only faster wheels. They give the sensation of direction while remaining inside the same horizontal motion. The residual path does not borrow a pole. It becomes one.

This solitary stance is the residual form of the older traditional knowledge of the axis. Stability was never only political. It was a quality of being. The man who keeps an unmoved center already performs the residual act. The turning of the age does not have to become the turning of the man.

Hold the polar stance. Do not let the speed of the field become the speed of your interior life. The residual act is to keep one point unmoved and to judge every motion from that point. That unmoved point is the last form of the pole.
