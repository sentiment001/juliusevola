Day 77 — The Forces of Dissolution

"The forces of dissolution are not merely destructive. They are also the expression of a cycle that has reached its terminal phase. To understand them is the first condition for riding them without being carried into the abyss." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

The forces of dissolution are the powers that break down every residual form of hierarchy, quality, and sacred limit. They appear as the acceleration of technology, the global economic machine, the mass democratic and egalitarian ideologies, the cult of perpetual novelty, and the progressive erasure of every distinction that once gave shape to human existence. These forces are not accidental. They belong to the nature of the present phase of the historical cycle.

The differentiated individual does not waste energy denying their existence or their strength. He studies them. He sees how they operate on the mass and how they seek to operate on him. The study is cold. Sentimentality about the past and outrage at the present both obscure the clear perception required. Only clear perception allows the man to locate the points at which the forces can be used rather than suffered.

Many young men feel the pressure of these forces as a personal oppression or as a call to immediate revolt. Both reactions keep them inside the logic of the dissolution. The first produces resignation or bitterness. The second produces theatrical opposition that the system easily absorbs or neutralizes. The rider of the tiger neither resigns nor revolts in the ordinary sense. He assesses the current and decides how to move with it while keeping his own orientation intact.

Understanding the forces of dissolution is therefore a form of self-defense and a form of preparation. The man who knows what is moving can avoid being surprised by it. He can also recognize when a particular current offers a temporary advantage that can be taken without surrender of the center. The next days will examine the practical implications of this understanding.
