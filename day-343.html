Day 343 — The Sacred Without Temple

"The sacred is a dimension of reality. It does not cease to exist because the temples that once housed it have been abandoned." — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

The sacred without temple is the capacity to keep a relation to the higher after every public dwelling of the sacred has been emptied or demolished. Temple, rite, and priesthood once made the sacred visible. Those forms have been hollowed or replaced by sentiment. The dimension itself remains. The man either stands in relation to it or he lives as if the world were only surface.

Young men who still feel the sacred often look for residual temples and find aesthetics, politics in vestments, or private mood. Mood is not the sacred. The residual path does not wait for a restored house. It treats certain acts, limits, and silences as the remaining points of contact with a higher order.

This solitary relation is the residual form of the older traditional knowledge of the holy. The holy was never identical with the building. The building was a support. The man who keeps reverence, limit, and vertical attention already performs the residual act. He does so without requiring the age to believe him.

Keep the sacred without a temple. Do not reduce it to feeling and do not postpone it until a rite returns. The residual act is to give certain hours, certain decisions, and certain refusals a higher character. That character is the last temple a man can still build.
