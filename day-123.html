Day 123 — Reading

"The book that is read under a higher measure becomes an instrument of formation. The same book read under the pressure of the age becomes only another form of consumption." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

Reading is not neutral. The manner in which a man approaches a text reveals the measure he is already living by. The horizontal man reads for information, for stimulation, or for the confirmation of positions he already holds. The differentiated individual reads in order to test and to strengthen the permanent standard. He does not collect opinions. He extracts what can be used for the formation of the type.

The age has made reading continuous and shallow. The young man is trained to move rapidly across surfaces and to treat depth as inefficiency. That training produces a mind that can absorb volume without being altered by it. The higher type recovers the capacity for sustained attention to a single demanding text. He reads slowly when the material requires it. He returns to the same pages. He allows the text to press against his current formation rather than using the text to decorate it.

Young men often treat the quantity of books finished as a measure of seriousness. The higher type treats the quality of the encounter with a single serious book as the real measure. A text that has been absorbed under the permanent standard changes the reader. A text that has been consumed under the pressure of novelty leaves the reader unchanged. The difference is visible in the subsequent decisions of the man, not in the list of titles he can recite.

Choose fewer books. Read them under the same discipline applied to the body and to speech. Refuse the continuous intake of secondary material that dilutes attention. The capacity to read with force is one of the practical supports of the differentiated individual in an age that prefers the rapid and the disposable.
