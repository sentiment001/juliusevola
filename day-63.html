Day 63 — Inner Hierarchy

"When the outer hierarchy has disappeared, the hierarchical principle must be realized within the individual himself. The higher elements of his being must rule the lower, or the man ceases to be differentiated." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

Inner hierarchy is the translation of the Traditional principle into the conditions of a late age. In a living Traditional order the hierarchy was outer as well as inner. Rank, function, and authority reflected a cosmic and spiritual order. That reflection has been shattered. What remains possible is the establishment of the same order inside the man who still recognizes it.

The work is concrete. The higher faculties of attention, of judgment, of deliberate will must govern the lower movements of appetite, of emotional reaction, of habitual dispersion. This is not a matter of repression for its own sake. It is a matter of rank. The lower has its place. It does not have the right to command. When it does command, the man becomes a field of competing forces rather than a center of order.

Most men never undertake this work with seriousness. The age encourages the opposite. It treats every impulse as legitimate, every desire as a right, every form of self-discipline as a residual pathology. The differentiated individual rejects that teaching. He knows that without an interior ranking of forces there is no self capable of standing against the dissolving pressures of the environment.

The establishment of inner hierarchy is daily and unfinished. It is tested in moments of fatigue, of temptation, of social pressure, of unexpected difficulty. Each test either strengthens the higher command or weakens it. Over time the man who persists acquires a different density. He is no longer merely a collection of reactions. He has become a structure. That structure is the inner hierarchy made real.
