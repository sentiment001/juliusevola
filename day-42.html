Day 42 — Initiation and the Path

"Initiation is the process by which a man is brought into contact with a higher order of reality and is transformed by that contact. It is the passage from the profane to the sacred, from the contingent to the absolute." — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

In Traditional civilizations initiation was a real and ordered process. It involved rites, trials, and a transmission of knowledge that was not available to the ordinary man. The initiate did not merely learn new facts. He underwent a change of state. He was made capable of participating in a reality that stood above the common world of birth, work, and death.

The modern world has no genuine initiatic institutions left in the public sphere. What remains under that name is either a commercial imitation or a private and often incomplete attempt. The young man who seeks a path of real transformation therefore faces a different condition. The outer supports are gone. The demand falls more heavily on the individual himself.

The essential content of initiation remains available. It is the recognition that a higher state of being is possible and the systematic effort to move toward it. That effort requires discipline of the body, clarity of the mind, and a refusal to treat the ordinary self as the final measure. The path is interior, yet it is not formless. It still answers to a hierarchy of qualities and to a standard that does not shift with mood or fashion.

The man who takes this seriously will discover that most of what the age offers as growth or self-improvement moves in the opposite direction. It multiplies experiences and softens the edges of the personality. The initiatic direction reduces the unnecessary and hardens the essential. The difference is not subtle. Once it is felt, the ordinary road becomes difficult to travel without a sense of loss.
