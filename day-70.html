Day 70 — The Long View

"The differentiated man must learn to think and to act according to a measure that exceeds the span of a single life and the horizon of the present age. Only the long view preserves him from the urgencies and the despair of the moment." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

The long view is the capacity to place the present condition of the world inside a larger cycle. The age of dissolution is not the first and it will not be the last. Traditional doctrine has always known periods of descent and periods of restoration. The differentiated individual does not require the restoration to occur in his lifetime in order to justify the higher orientation. The orientation is valid independently of the outer phase.

This view protects against two equal errors. The first is the frantic activism that treats every current event as decisive and demands immediate, total response. The second is the collapse into resignation that treats the present darkness as final and therefore excuses every surrender. Both errors remain trapped inside the horizon of the age. The long view steps outside that horizon.

In practice the long view appears as patience, as the refusal to be stampeded by the latest crisis or the latest cultural shift, as the steady continuation of the inner work regardless of outer weather. It also appears as a certain indifference to the judgment of contemporaries. The man who measures himself by a standard that outlasts the present generation has already begun to free himself from the need for its approval.

The differentiated individual does not know what will follow the present phase. He does not need to know. His task is to remain the type of man in whom the higher principle continues to act. If such men persist, something of the vertical order is preserved even when the outer forms have vanished. That preservation is the only contribution that still matters. The long view makes it possible.
