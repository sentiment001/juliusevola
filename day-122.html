Day 122 — Silence

"Silence is not the absence of speech. It is the presence of a measure that does not require continuous expression in order to remain real." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

Silence is a positive capacity. The man who cannot remain silent without discomfort has not yet established interior distance. He requires the continuous confirmation of his own voice or of external noise. The differentiated individual can inhabit silence without the need to fill it. That capacity is not cultivated by long retreats. It is cultivated by the repeated refusal to speak when speech is unnecessary and by the refusal to seek noise when the environment is quiet.

The age saturates every interval with sound and with the demand for response. The young man who accepts that saturation loses the ability to hear himself. Speech becomes reactive. Thought becomes fragmented. The higher type recovers intervals of silence as a practical necessity. He does not announce them. He simply protects them. In those intervals the permanent measure can be felt without the interference of the continuous horizontal current.

Young men are trained to interpret silence as awkwardness or as a lack of social skill. The higher type interprets the inability to tolerate silence as a lack of formation. The man who must speak in order to confirm his existence has placed his existence in the hands of the external. The man who can remain silent without loss of presence has already established a center that does not depend on continuous expression.

Practice the refusal of unnecessary speech. Practice the acceptance of quiet intervals without reaching for a device or for conversation. The capacity for silence is one of the clearest external signs that the interior work has begun to take hold. Without it the other applications remain incomplete.
