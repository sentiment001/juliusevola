Day 57 — Interior Distance

"The essential thing is to maintain an interior distance from everything that constitutes the substance of the modern world. This distance is the condition of freedom and of any higher form of action." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

Interior distance is the first practical requirement of the differentiated individual. It is the capacity to participate in the outer life of the age while remaining unclaimed by its values, its urgencies, and its emotional climate. The man who lacks this distance is carried by every current. The man who possesses it can move through the same currents without being dissolved by them.

The distance is interior. It does not require physical withdrawal or the rejection of ordinary duties. It requires a clear separation between the surface of life and the center that judges that surface. Events, opinions, pleasures, and pressures arrive. They are registered. They are not allowed to set the measure of the man. The measure remains the higher one he has already recognized.

Most young men are trained against this distance. The age teaches identification. Feel everything. Belong to every cause. React at once. The man who begins to hold himself back appears cold or arrogant. That appearance is the price of the distance. He must pay it without complaint and without the need to justify himself to those who live only on the surface.

Without interior distance there is no differentiated individual. There is only the man who is still formed by the environment he claims to resist. The work of establishing that distance is daily and unspectacular. It consists in the repeated refusal to let the age define what is important, what is urgent, and what is real.
