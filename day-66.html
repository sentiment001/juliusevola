Day 66 — Remaining Standing

"The essential task is to remain standing. In a world that tends toward dissolution and leveling, the simple fact of continuing to stand according to a higher measure already constitutes a form of victory." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

Remaining standing is the minimal and the maximal demand of the differentiated individual. Minimal because it does not require the reconstruction of a lost world or the successful transformation of the surrounding age. Maximal because, under present conditions, the pressure to yield is constant and the supports that once made standing natural have been removed. To continue is already an achievement of rank.

The age does not usually attack the higher type with open force. It works through fatigue, through isolation, through the slow accumulation of small surrenders, through the suggestion that the higher measure is unrealistic or outdated. The man who remains standing must therefore watch for these quieter forms of pressure. He must recognize them when they appear and refuse them without drama.

Standing is not a single decision. It is a posture maintained across time. It includes the body, the attention, the use of the day, the quality of what is allowed to enter the mind. Each of these is a point at which the man either continues to stand or begins to lean. The differentiated individual treats every such point as significant. There is no neutral ground.

Those who look for spectacular results will find this emphasis unsatisfying. The age itself trains the expectation of visible transformation. The differentiated individual has already abandoned that expectation. His concern is the integrity of the center under conditions that seek to dissolve it. If that center remains intact, he has remained standing. Everything else is secondary.
