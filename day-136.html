Day 136 — Refusal of False Centers

"In the absence of a true center the age multiplies substitutes, each of which claims to orient the man while binding him more tightly to the plane of dissolution." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

False centers appear wherever the genuine vertical orientation has been withdrawn. Political movements, lifestyle identities, therapeutic systems, and various forms of collective belonging all offer the appearance of direction. Each of them operates on the horizontal plane. They organize the man's energies around temporary goals and temporary affiliations. They do not restore the axis that would make those energies serve a higher measure.

Young men who sense the emptiness of the age are especially vulnerable to these substitutes. The need for orientation is real. The substitutes exploit that need. They provide a sense of belonging and a ready-made language of meaning. The cost is the quiet surrender of interior sovereignty. The man who accepts a false center no longer tests his actions against the permanent measure. He tests them against the requirements of the group or the current doctrine.

The differentiated individual refuses the substitutes without making a spectacle of the refusal. He does not need to denounce every false center in public. He simply declines to orient himself by them. The refusal is maintained in the ordinary decisions of speech, association, and attention. Over time the interior axis becomes more stable precisely because it is no longer distracted by the claims of the substitutes.

Examine the centers toward which your attention and energy are drawn. Ask whether they reinforce the permanent measure or whether they quietly replace it. The capacity to recognize and refuse false centers is itself a residual possibility. It is exercised in the man who prefers the difficulty of true orientation to the ease of temporary belonging.
