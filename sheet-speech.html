Speech

Residual Application Sheet
"Speech is the domain in which interior order either appears or collapses into the horizontal." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

Speech is the place where the interior hierarchy becomes audible. In a traditional order the word carried rank. It was measured because it expressed a distinction between what was higher and what was lower, between what was necessary and what was merely reactive. The modern age dissolves that measure. Talk becomes continuous, opinion becomes interchangeable with knowledge, and the refusal to speak is treated as a failure of participation. The differentiated individual recovers the older restraint. He speaks when the word serves the measure. He remains silent when speech would only maintain the horizontal current or soften a distinction the age prefers to blur.

The common failures are familiar. The man speaks to keep the room intact, to avoid the discomfort of a clear line, or to supply the reaction the moment seems to require. He adds the unnecessary sentence that signals agreement with the prevailing tone. He treats silence as awkward rather than as the residual form of measure. Each of these concessions is small. Together they train the word to follow the path of least resistance. The interior order that cannot command speech under ordinary conditions will not command it when the pressure increases. The age rewards the man who remains verbally available. Availability without measure is already a form of dissolution.

The practice is measured speech under ordinary conditions. For a defined period the man refuses unnecessary talk. He does not fill silence. He does not soften a distinction in order to preserve ease. When he speaks, the word is required by the situation rather than by the habit of participation. The period can be a single conversation, an evening, or a sequence of days. The test is whether the refusal holds when the easiest movement is to continue speaking. Measured speech is not theatrical reticence. It is the ordinary capacity to let the word remain under command.

This restraint is the residual form of the older knowledge that speech either expresses rank or abandons it. No collective order now confirms the restraint or penalizes its absence. The man who keeps the word measured already performs the residual act. The act does not require an audience that recognizes it. It requires only that the interior hierarchy continue to govern the domain in which it is most easily lost to reaction and to the desire for inclusion.
