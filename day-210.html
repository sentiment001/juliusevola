Day 210 — Carrying the Measure

"The residual task is to carry the permanent measure when every external structure that once carried it has been dissolved by the cycle." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

The task now is to carry the permanent measure after every external structure that once carried it has been dissolved. Rank, rite, and sacred authority once held the criterion in public, so that a man of quality met it outside himself. Those structures are gone. He does not invent a lighter standard to match the climate. He continues to live by the same vertical criterion, and the weight that institutions once shared is now his.

Carrying is a concrete labor, and it shows in ordinary domains. The body stays under a rule, speech stays under measure, and desire is refused its claim to govern the day. Time is spent as if it belonged to something higher than appetite and display. None of this is a private mood. It is the late form of the older hierarchical work, done without the collective expression that once made the work visible, and the measure does not change because the carriers have withdrawn.

Young men feel the weight as a continuous temptation to set it down, or to find a group that will share it. Setting it down is collapse, and the age will call the collapse realism: belonging restored, softness praised, the job that rewards only output accepted as the whole life. The group is the subtler leak. Most collectives of this age will share the weight only after the measure has been lowered to a level they can applaud. The hunger to be seen makes that lowering look like fellowship.

Carry it into the next choice that will not be shared. Decide how the body is used tonight, what is spoken, what company is kept, what is refused without a speech. If you need a circle to hold the line with you, you have already set the measure down. A restored order would take part of the weight. Keep it through the hour that offers to take it off your hands.
