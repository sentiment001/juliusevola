Day 336 — The Peak Without a Range

"The mountain is the symbol of a state of being. Even when the sacred landscape has vanished, the peak remains as an interior direction." — Julius Evola, Meditations on the Peaks

The peak without a range is the capacity to pursue height after the mountain chain of Tradition has disappeared from the public world. In earlier ages the peak stood inside a range: rites, ranks, and sacred geography that made ascent visible. Those ranges have been leveled. The peak remains as an interior direction. The man who still climbs does so without a public mountain.

Young men who feel the pull of height often look for residual ranges: schools, orders, scenes that promise a landscape of ascent. Most of those landscapes are already horizontal. They offer company and language. They do not offer altitude. The residual path does not wait for a range. It treats the peak as a demand that can be answered from a single life.

This solitary ascent is the residual form of the older traditional knowledge of the mountain. Height was never only geography. It was a state of being. The man who keeps that state decisive already performs the residual act. The missing range does not cancel the peak.

Climb without a range. Do not postpone height until the age restores a sacred landscape. The residual act is to keep the upward measure present in body, work, and solitude. That presence is the peak under residual conditions.
