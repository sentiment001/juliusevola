Day 25 — Nihilism as the Air of the Age

"Nihilism is the air that the modern world breathes. It is the progressive dissolution of every absolute value, every higher meaning, and every form that once stood above the pure flux of becoming." — Julius Evola, Ride the Tiger

Nihilism is rarely announced as a doctrine. It operates as the unspoken assumption that nothing finally matters beyond the satisfaction of desire, the avoidance of pain, and the management of appearances. Higher claims are treated as personal constructions. When they lose their force, the only remaining reality is the play of forces without center or goal.

Evola saw that the modern process leads inevitably to this condition. Once the vertical dimension is rejected, once authority is reduced to human convention, the path ends in the recognition that every value is provisional. Many young men sense this without being able to name it. They feel the emptiness beneath the official languages of progress, inclusion, and self-expression. That feeling is not a personal failure. It is the accurate perception of the air of the age.

The danger is to treat the emptiness as final and to make a lifestyle of it. Some respond with irony, others with pure appetite, others with a search for intensity that never reaches beyond the horizontal. The differentiated man must go further. He must see the nihilism clearly and then refuse to let it become the ground of his own existence. The refusal is not a return to comforting beliefs. It is the decision to live as if a higher measure still exists and to form himself according to that measure.

This is the point at which the diagnosis becomes practical. The man who can stand inside a nihilistic environment without being dissolved by it has already begun the work that Ride the Tiger describes. He does not need the world to confirm his standard. He maintains it from within.
