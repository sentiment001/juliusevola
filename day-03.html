Day 3 — Quality and Quantity

"Modern society presents precisely a type of organism that has passed from the human to the subhuman, in which every activity and every reaction is determined by the needs and inclinations of a purely corporeal life. Its dominant principles are exactly those that are proper to the animal and organic-vital part of traditional hierarchies: gold and labor." — Julius Evola, Revolt Against the Modern World

The modern world measures almost everything by quantity. More people, more goods, more information, more choices, more years of life. Numbers become the main proof of value. Bigger is treated as better. This habit of mind is so complete that most people never notice it operating.

A Traditional outlook begins from the opposite direction. What matters first is quality: the rank, the intensity, the level of being. One higher type of man carries more weight than a crowd of lower ones. One clear principle is worth more than a mass of opinions. When quantity rules, the higher is always dragged down to the average. When quality is primary, the average is expected to orient itself upward.

You will be told that this way of thinking is elitist or cruel. The accusation itself is a product of the quantitative view. It assumes that the only fair arrangement is one in which nothing is allowed to stand higher. The charge is useful only if you already accept the premise it rests on. Once you see the premise clearly, the accusation loses most of its force.

The work here is to recover the ability to rank. That ability has been systematically discouraged. Recovering it is part of the orientation these pages aim at.
